HOW DOES PPS WORK?

PPS is a context-adjusted winrate. Wins earned during harder observed hours count more.

PPS measures public-game performance—not permanent skill.

THE IDEA IN THREE STEPS

From earlier games to PPS

1

Estimate the players

Only earlier observations estimate how often each player would normally win.

2

Rate the hour

Expected and actual results are compared. If observed players win less than expected, the hour appears harder.

3

Calculate PPS

Wins are multiplied by their hour’s Lobby Factor and divided by all rated games.

Previous games→Expected results→Hour difficulty→Lobby Factor→PPS

THE COMPLETE METHODShow every formula, variable and example

This is the exact method used by GooberStats. Each step includes the formula, its variables, why it exists and a simple example.

1

Expected Winrate before an hour

We estimate normal performance using only information available before the rated hour begins.

Historical weight = min(300 − Grecent, Ghistory) × 2−age / 365Expected WR = (Wrecent + Historical weight × WRhistory + Prior games × WRpopulation)
÷ (Grecent + Historical weight + Prior games)Expected WR is limited to 2%–98%.

Variables

  • G_recent / W_recent: up to the previous 300 observed games and wins.
  • G_history / WR_history: older profile evidence for returning players.
  • age: days since that evidence; its weight halves every 365 days.
  • Prior games: 40 for a known returning player, otherwise 100.
  • WR_population: median historical winrate of established observed players.

Why: Ten wins are less surprising for a player who usually wins 70% than one who usually wins 20%. The rated hour can never change its own expectation.

Example: 60 wins in the previous 200 games provide a 30% recent baseline before history and priors are applied.

2

Raw Hourly Effect

Expected results are compared with wins actually observed in that hour.

qi(δ) = logistic(logit(Expected WRi) + δ)Choose δ so that Σ Gamesi × qi(δ) = Actual WinsI = Σ Gamesi × qi(δ) × (1 − qi(δ))

Variables

  • i: one observed player.
  • δ: raw shift that matches expected results to actual wins.
  • q_i: adjusted expected probability for that player.
  • I: useful information in the window.

Why: Players are weighted by games played. A negative δ means the hour appears harder.

Example: If the field was expected to earn 40 wins but earned only 25, the fitted effect becomes negative.

3

Confidence and smoothing

A noisy hour is pulled toward its stable hour-of-day pattern; a well-observed hour keeps more of its own result.

λ = τ² ÷ (τ² + 1 ÷ (8 × I))Smoothed effect s = Hour prior + λ × (δ − Hour prior)

Variables

  • λ: how much the individual window is trusted.
  • τ²: estimated real variance between windows.
  • I: information from the previous step.
  • Hour prior: stable long-term pattern for that time of day.

Why: One strange result with little information should not create a record bonus. Confidence is very low below 10, low from 10, medium from 25 and high from 50.

Example: With λ = 0.80, the measured window supplies 80% of the adjustment.

4

Lobby Factor

The easiest stable observed hour-of-day is the zero-bonus reference. Harder effects produce a larger factor.

Lobby Bonus% = max(0, (eEasiest reference − s − 1) × 100)Lobby Factor = 1 + Lobby Bonus% ÷ 100

Variables

  • s: smoothed effect of this hourly window.
  • Easiest reference: zero-bonus baseline.
  • Lobby Bonus: adjustment shown as a percentage.
  • Lobby Factor: the same adjustment as a multiplier.

Why: The factor turns measured hourly difficulty into a weight for wins. It belongs to an observed hour, not one specific lobby.

Example: A +40% Lobby Bonus equals a ×1.40 Lobby Factor.

5

Public Performance Score (PPS)

Every observed win is multiplied by its hour’s Lobby Factor. Losses remain in the denominator.

Weighted Winsh = Winsh × Lobby FactorhPPS = 1000 × Σ Weighted Winsh ÷ Σ Gamesh

Monthly and Alltime

  • Monthly PPS: all rated games in the selected month; minimum 30.
  • Alltime PPS: exactly the latest 300 rated games.

Why: The exact formula remembers when wins occurred, so it is not always Monthly WR × average Lobby Factor × 10.

Example: 242.6 bonus-weighted wins across 380 games produce 1000 × 242.6 ÷ 380 = 638 PPS.

READ THIS BEFORE COMPARING PLAYERS

What PPS can—and cannot—measure

GooberStats observes public profile changes during scheduled hourly snapshots. Current coverage is approximately — of scheduled snapshots in the evaluated period. That does not mean the same percentage of all matches or players was observed.

Observed hours are not individual lobbies

We do not know the exact players sharing each match. Lobby Factor rates the public field observed during an hour.

Play styles are different

Some Goobers maximize safe wins. Others experiment, joke around, help friends, skip rounds or take risks. Lower PPS can still coexist with greater mechanical skill.

Fair play cannot be determined

Friends can help someone win. Alt accounts can smurf or sabotage another player. Public totals do not reliably reveal why a result happened.

Unknown players and missing hours

Not every profile, bot or game is visible. Missing snapshots and unknown profiles introduce uncertainty.

Confidence depends on the data

Small or unusual windows are smoothed more strongly. Confidence measures useful information, not whether players are trustworthy.

Performance is not permanent skill

PPS asks how strong the wins we observed were. It does not prove who would always win a fair match.
